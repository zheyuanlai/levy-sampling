\begin{abstract}
This document is a standalone rendering of the numerical-experiment section of
the L\'evy score-corrected compound-Poisson (LSC-CP) sampling paper, together
with its appendix.  Every number, table, and figure is generated from one
audited production campaign (immutable run directories
\texttt{20260717-prod-gpu7-\{A2,B2,C3\}}; commit \texttt{687c788}, working tree
recorded dirty) that passed all fail-closed numerical gates: weak-stationarity
certificates, timestep and quadrature refinement, nonfinite/clipping/basin-map
audits, and reference diagnostics.  Four systems are studied: the
one-dimensional double well $V(x)=(x^2-1)^2$, a forty-mode planar Gaussian
mixture, a depth-balanced three-well M\"uller--Brown landscape embedded in ten
dimensions, and a coupled $\phi^4$ chain in twenty-four dimensions.  LSC-CP is
the only method that reaches the finite-sample reference floor of the
distributional metrics on all four systems while sustaining nonzero
between-basin effective sample sizes; the three local baselines record exactly
zero between-basin ESS on every system.  All comparisons are equilibrium
sampling diagnostics; no physical kinetics is claimed.
\end{abstract}

\section{Overview and compared methods}
\label{sec:overview}

All experiments target a Boltzmann law
\begin{equation}
 \piB(x)=Z_\beta^{-1}e^{-\beta V(x)},\qquad x\in\R^d .
 \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
The sampler of interest augments overdamped Langevin dynamics with a finite
compound-Poisson jump process of measure $\nu(\dd r)=\lambda\rho(\dd r)$ and
adds the stationary L\'evy-score drift $S_{\nu,\beta}$ so that the combined
generator
\begin{equation}
 A_{\nu,\beta}f
 =\bigl[-\nabla V+S_{\nu,\beta}\bigr]\cdot\nabla f
  +\beta^{-1}\Delta f+J_\nu f
 \label{eq:corrected-generator}
\end{equation}
is weakly stationary for $\piB$ (Appendix~\ref{app:construction}).  The
practical estimators evaluate the score integral by deterministic chord
quadrature where affordable and by \emph{paired} random-bank estimators in
higher dimension: the realized jump displacements and the L\'evy-score drift of
one step are generated from the same random finite measure, so the
drift--jump cancellation holds conditionally on every realized bank
(Appendix~\ref{app:paired}).  Figures label the deployed estimator simply
\textsc{lsc-cp}; run manifests record the exact variant.  The deployed
variants are: deterministic shell quadrature (double well), a closed-form
score (MoG40), the paired single-atom estimator \textsc{lsc-cp-ra} (double
well and MoG40, as an estimator-agreement check), and the paired multi-atom
estimator \textsc{lsc-cp-ma} (M\"uller--Brown and $\phi^4$).

Baselines are the unadjusted Langevin algorithm (\textsc{ula}), Metropolis
adjusted Langevin (\textsc{mala}), the BAOAB underdamped integrator
(\textsc{baoab}), an $\alpha$-stable L\'evy-flight analogue (\textsc{fla}),
and parallel tempering (\textsc{pt}) with an automatically tuned ladder and
fully counted replica cost.  Raw compound Poisson (\textsc{cp}), i.e.\ the
same jump bank \emph{without} the score correction, is included as a
stationarity-defect ablation: its invariant law is not $\piB$, so it is a
control for the value of the correction, not a fair efficiency baseline.  All
methods share per-seed initial ensembles; \textsc{cp} and full-law
\textsc{lsc-cp} additionally share the jump random stream pathwise.

Two claims are \emph{not} made.  First, no statement about physical reaction
kinetics: the jump bank, score drift, taming, and algorithmic clock are
designed for equilibrium sampling, and passage statistics compare algorithmic
mixing only.  Second, no claim that the finite-step chain preserves $\piB$
exactly: the paired construction is exact at continuous-generator level, and
the remaining finite-$\Delta t$ error is measured by the refinement and audit
gates of Section~\ref{sec:protocol} rather than assumed away.

\paragraph{Double well ($d=1$).}
$V(x)=(x^2-1)^2$ at $\beta=8$: two equal wells at $\pm1$ separated by a
dimensionless barrier $\beta\Delta V=8$.  The jump bank is a symmetric shell
at $\pm2$ with radial half-width $0.2$.  This is the transparent two-state
test of target recovery and interwell communication.

\paragraph{MoG40 ($d=2$).}
$\piB$ is an equal-weight mixture of forty unit-covariance Gaussians;
$V(x)=-\beta^{-1}\log\sum_{k}\exp[-\lVert x-\mu_k\rVert^2/2]$.  The jump law
is deliberately generic: radius uniform on $[4,15]$ (set from the
nearest-neighbour distance histogram alone), direction uniform on the circle.
Neither \textsc{pt} nor \textsc{lsc-cp} receives the mode locations.  This is
the many-basin stress test.

\paragraph{Depth-balanced M\"uller--Brown ($d=10$).}
A M\"uller--Brown-type three-well landscape whose well depths are retuned to
be equal, evaluated in a two-dimensional latent plane and embedded in ten
dimensions by a fixed linear map with Gaussian auxiliary coordinates
($\sigma_{\rm aux}=0.4$), at $\beta=24$.  The equal depths separate
multimodality from metastability: the channels retain dimensionless barriers
$\beta b_{AB}\simeq11.1$ and $\beta b_{BC}\simeq4.0$ while the equilibrium
masses stay comparable.  The bank has four directed relay atoms
$A\leftrightarrow B\leftrightarrow C$ (no direct $A$--$C$ atom), so transport
must use the relay through $B$.  This tests curved, nonuniform basins and a
designed jump graph.

\paragraph{Coupled $\phi^4$ chain ($d=24$).}
Twelve periodic two-component sites,
$V(q)=\tfrac{\kappa}{2\delta}\sum_i\lVert q_{i+1}-q_i\rVert^2
+\delta\sum_iW(q_i)$ with $\kappa=2.5$, $\delta=1/12$, $\beta=8$, and a tilted
double-double-well site potential $W$ (Appendix~\ref{app:specs}).  The target
has four coherent phases; the bank has eight directed edge atoms along the
phase square (diagonals excluded because their coherent chords cross the
field-zero hilltop).  The primary collective variable is the spatial mean
$\bar q=\tfrac1{12}\sum_iq_i\in\R^2$.  This is the many-body example: a
collective transition in a coupled field.

The four systems form a hierarchy of equilibrium questions---two-state
balance, many-mode occupancy, curved-basin relay transport, and a collective
field transition---rather than four independent leaderboard tasks.

\section{Protocol, references, and validation gates}
\label{sec:protocol}

\paragraph{Run protocol.}
For each system all seeds are batched into one ensemble per method with
per-seed initial blocks; every method starts from the identical localized
per-seed ensemble.  The timestep is selected by a fail-closed halving rule:
the largest dyadic $\Delta t$ at which every $\piB$-targeting method's
terminal metrics agree with their $\Delta t/2$ values within declared
floor-guarded tolerances (\textsc{fla} and \textsc{cp}, whose invariant laws
are not $\piB$, are recorded but do not gate).  Score quadrature is selected
by the analogous rule with an additional weak-stationarity certificate
threshold.  Exhausting either refinement grid aborts the run; it does not
default to the finest attempted setting.

\paragraph{References and floors.}
The double well uses a dense-grid inverse-CDF reference; MoG40 an exact
mixture draw; M\"uller--Brown a fine two-dimensional latent grid combined
with exact Gaussian auxiliaries; $\phi^4$ a Laplace-mixture proposal corrected
by self-normalized importance weights (direct SNIS for basin masses, energy
moments, CV means, and the free-energy surface; finite
sampling-importance-resampling only where unweighted clouds are required).
The $\phi^4$ weight diagnostics for the production reference are: $2\times
10^5$ proposals, weight ESS $41{,}182$ (fraction $0.206$), maximum normalized
weight $3.6\times10^{-3}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:references}).  For every metric,
a finite-sample \emph{floor} is estimated by comparing two independent
reference draws of the same size; ``at the floor'' below means within the
floor band $\text{mean}+3\,\text{sd}$.

\paragraph{Gates.}
A run is accepted only if: the pre-production certificate residual is below
$10^{-6}$ on a domain extending at least one maximum jump beyond the
high-probability region; refinements pass; nonfinite counts are exactly zero;
score-exponent and state-box clipping stay below declared tolerances;
jump-boundary contact of the $\piB$-targeting jump methods stays below
$10^{-2}$ per applied jump; and the basin-map-domain escape fraction of
targeting methods stays below $10^{-3}$.  All thresholds, measured values,
and the two domain-coverage design rules they enforce are reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:gates}.  Every run writes its requested and resolved
configurations, certificate, refinement tables, diagnostics, logs, and
outputs into an immutable timestamped directory
(Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).

Four observations organize Table~\ref{tab:terminal} and
Figure~\ref{fig:relaxation}.

\emph{(i) Local samplers do not leave their initial basin.}
On every system, \textsc{ula}, \textsc{mala}, and \textsc{baoab} remain
essentially where they started for the entire horizon (basin TV
$0.31$--$0.93$, basin KL $1.1$--$4.2$).  At these dimensionless barriers this
is expected and quantifies the problem the nonlocal methods must solve.

\emph{(ii) LSC-CP reaches the floor on all four systems.}
The deployed LSC-CP estimator attains basin TV and KL at or within the
finite-sample floor band on the double well, M\"uller--Brown, and $\phi^4$,
and comes within $25\%$ of the MoG40 TV floor while every alternative is at
least $2.2\times$ further away.  It is the only method with this property.
Parallel tempering matches it on the double well and M\"uller--Brown but is
$4.6\times$ worse in basin KL on MoG40 ($0.055$ vs $0.012$) and $26\times$
worse on $\phi^4$ ($0.039$ vs $0.0015$), at full replica cost.  The
$\alpha$-stable flight is competitive only on the double well; its invariant
law is visibly biased elsewhere (MB free-energy RMSE $0.94\,k_{\rm B}T$).

\emph{(iii) The score correction, not the jumps alone, closes the defect.}
Raw \textsc{cp} shares the bank, jump times, and jump increments with
\textsc{lsc-cp} pathwise, yet retains a large stationary defect: basin TV
$0.286/0.135/0.077$ on MoG40/MB/$\phi^4$ and, most tellingly, $17$--$25\%$ of
its terminal mass falls outside the reference free-energy grid on the three
multivariate systems, versus $\le0.6\%$ for LSC-CP.  Its boundary-contact
rate---$2.5\%$, $6.7\%$, and $0.4\%$ of applied jumps on MoG40, MB, and
$\phi^4$---is reported as part of this defect (Appendix~\ref{app:gates}).  We
note for fairness that an earlier tight $\phi^4$ integration box
$[-2,2]^{24}$ had \emph{flattered} raw \textsc{cp} by confining its spread;
with the audited box $[-5,5]^{24}$ its $W_2$ moves from $0.28$ to $0.41$
while LSC-CP-\textsc{ma} is unchanged ($0.099$ vs $0.100$), evidence that the
corrected sampler is insensitive to the numerical box while the uncorrected
one is not.

\emph{(iv) Paired estimators agree with the deterministic score.}
Where both are run (double well, MoG40), the paired single-atom estimator
tracks the deterministic-quadrature score within a factor of $1.6$ on
every metric and with the same qualitative ordering (e.g.\ MoG40 basin KL
$0.036$ vs $0.012$; double-well $W_2$ $0.131$ vs $0.111$), supporting the use
of the cheaper paired multi-atom estimator on the two systems where
deterministic quadrature is unaffordable.

\section{Stationary sampling efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}

Terminal ensemble metrics answer ``how close is the law at time $T$''; they
do not measure the precision of long-run time averages.  For that, small
independent chains (4 seeds $\times$ 8 chains, 1000 uniformly spaced draws
over one production horizon) are initialized from the system reference,
charged one full settling horizon, and analyzed for basin indicators, energy,
and the collective variables.  Every trace is labelled with its
initialization provenance; none is claimed to be an exact stationary draw of
the discrete kernel, so all values below are post-settling diagnostics of the
numerical chains (Appendix~\ref{app:metrics}).  Chains with zero observed
transitions contribute zero ESS by construction; a trapped chain is reported
as trapped, not as converged.  Raw \textsc{cp} and \textsc{fla} are excluded
because their invariant laws are not $\piB$.

Three conclusions follow from Table~\ref{tab:ess}.

\emph{(i) Between-basin ESS separates the methods qualitatively.}
The three local integrators record \emph{exactly zero} between-basin ESS on
every system: over one full production horizon at stationarity, no chain
crossed a barrier even once.  Their large energy ESS values illustrate why
energy decorrelation alone must never be quoted as convergence evidence.
LSC-CP sustains worst-basin ESS between $229$ and $1{,}952$---the same order
as fully costed parallel tempering---using only local gradients plus the
jump-bank score.

\emph{(ii) Mixing and accuracy must be read together.}
On MoG40 and $\phi^4$, \textsc{pt} shows the larger between-basin ESS
($572$ vs $229$; $2{,}567$ vs $1{,}150$) yet its terminal occupancy accuracy
is a factor $4.6$--$26$ \emph{worse} in basin KL (Table~\ref{tab:terminal}).
Tempered swaps relabel basins quickly, but the resulting cold-chain law is
less accurate than the score-corrected chain at matched horizons.  Neither
column alone ranks the samplers; the product of accuracy and per-cost mixing
does.

\emph{(iii) The LSC chains carry a measurable, honest energy bias.}
The stationary energy mean of the LSC chains exceeds the target by $+0.04$ to
$+0.25$ energy units (many standard errors), while local and \textsc{pt}
chains are unbiased at the $10^{-3}$ level.  This is the expected signature
of the finite-step chain's invariant law: a jump deposits a particle on a
high-energy chord point and the tamed drift relaxes it over a finite time, so
an $O(\lambda\tau_{\rm relax})$ fraction of stationary time is spent in
elevated-energy transients.  The distributional metrics of
Table~\ref{tab:terminal} include exactly these transients and still reach the
reference floors, which bounds the practical impact; nevertheless every ESS
or throughput number for LSC-CP must be displayed next to this bias, and is,
throughout the released data.

\emph{Cost-normalized throughput.}
Per-second worst-basin throughput on the two cleanly timed systems (single
tenant on one H200 GPU) is the same order for LSC-CP and \textsc{pt}: on MB,
$33$ vs $48$ ESS/s (energy observable) with LSC-CP paying $A q_\theta=64$
score-quadrature energy differences per particle step; on $\phi^4$, $51$ vs
$62$ ESS/s with $Aq_\theta=256$.  The double-well and MoG40 timing columns
were collected while a second job shared the GPU and are reported in the
manifests with that caveat; per-gradient, per-potential, and
per-score-quadrature ESS, which are hardware independent, are stored per
observable in the released stationarity tables
(Appendix~\ref{app:provenance}).

\section{Scope, negative controls, and outlook}
\label{sec:outlook}

Three boundaries of the present evidence are stated explicitly.  First, all
results are equilibrium-sampling diagnostics of algorithmic chains; passage
or round-trip statistics, where recorded, are Kaplan--Meier restricted means
of algorithmic mixing times and carry no molecular-kinetics meaning.  Second,
the finite-step LSC chains are not exactly $\piB$-invariant, and the measured
consequences---the energy-transient bias of Section~\ref{sec:efficiency} and
the audited clipping/boundary diagnostics of Appendix~\ref{app:gates}---are
part of the reported results rather than suppressed.  Third, one planned
ablation is not yet included: a matched-length \emph{random-geometry} jump
control (same atom count, length multiset, weights, rate, and cost; randomized
directions) that would isolate the contribution of the designed bank geometry
from the generic value of long jumps; raw \textsc{cp} controls for the score
correction, not for geometry.  A $\phi^4$ box-sensitivity confirmation at
$L=6,7$ and an independent (non-SNIS) $\phi^4$ reference cross-check beyond
the long \textsc{pt} run are planned for the same reason.  These are
completions of the control matrix, not changes to the sampler or claims.

\section{The L\'evy score and the paired-bank estimators}
\label{app:construction}

For a finite jump measure $\nu$, the jump generator is
$(J_\nu f)(x)=\int[f(x+r)-f(x)]\,\nu(\dd r)$, and the L\'evy score is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\nu,\beta}(x)
 =-\int_{\R^d\setminus\{0\}} r\int_0^1
   \frac{\piB(x-\theta r)}{\piB(x)}\,\dd\theta\,\nu(\dd r)
 =-\int r\int_0^1
   e^{\beta[V(x)-V(x-\theta r)]}\,\dd\theta\,\nu(\dd r).
 \label{eq:levy-score}
\end{equation}
The chord fundamental theorem of calculus gives, for smooth compactly
supported $f$,
\begin{equation}
 \int S_{\nu,\beta}(x)\cdot\nabla f(x)\,\piB(x)\dd x
 =-\int (J_\nu f)(y)\,\piB(y)\dd y,
 \label{eq:weak-cancel}
\end{equation}
hence $\int A_{\nu,\beta}f\,\piB\dd x=0$ for the generator
\eqref{eq:corrected-generator}: an infinitesimal, continuous-time weak
stationarity statement.

For a finite bank
$\nu_{\mathcal R}(\dd r)=\lambda\sum_{a=1}^{A}w_a\delta_{R_a}(\dd r)$ the
score is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathcal R,\beta}(x)
 =-\lambda\sum_{a=1}^{A}w_aR_a I_a(x),\qquad
 I_a(x)=\int_0^1e^{\beta[V(x)-V(x-\theta R_a)]}\dd\theta,
 \label{eq:finite-bank-score}
\end{equation}
with $I_a$ evaluated by Gauss--Legendre quadrature in $\theta$ (and, for the
deterministic shell score, in the radial coordinate).  The implementation
accumulates the atom sum in log space with one \emph{global} maximum exponent
extracted after the signed-vector accumulation; capping that single global
exponent preserves the relative atom weights, whereas per-atom capping would
no longer compute the score of the realized measure.  The cap activation
fraction and maximum pre-cap exponent are recorded diagnostics
(Appendix~\ref{app:gates}).

\subsection{Paired random banks}
\label{app:paired}

When the intended law is a mixture of conditional atom laws,
$\nu(\dd r)=\lambda\sum_aw_aq_a(\dd r)$, the paired multi-atom estimator
draws, at the start of each step and independently of the state,
\begin{equation}
 R_{n,a}\sim q_a,
 \qquad
 \widehat\nu_n(\dd r)=\lambda\sum_{a=1}^{A}w_a\delta_{R_{n,a}}(\dd r),
 \qquad \E[\widehat\nu_n]=\nu ,
\end{equation}
evaluates the score \eqref{eq:finite-bank-score} of the \emph{realized} bank,
and jumps with atomwise counts drawn conditionally on that same bank,
\begin{equation}
 N_{n,a}\sim\operatorname{Poisson}(\lambda w_a\Delta t),
 \qquad
 \Delta J_n=\sum_{a=1}^{A}N_{n,a}R_{n,a}.
 \label{eq:paired-counts}
\end{equation}
Conditional on every frozen bank, drift and jumps realize the same measure
$\widehat\nu_n$, so \eqref{eq:weak-cancel} holds bank by bank at generator
level; refreshing the state-independent bank averages back to $\nu$.  If
instead the jumps were drawn from the marginal law while the score used an
independently realized bank, the conditional defect
$\int[J_{\widehat\nu}f-J_{\nu}f]\,\piB\dd x$ would generally be nonzero:
unbiasedness of the score alone is not a substitute for conditional
matching.  The production sampler enforces the pairing structurally (the bank
tensor is drawn once, used by both operators, then discarded) and its
constructor rejects any score/jump configuration whose law object, rate,
temperature, device, or precision disagree.  The single-atom variant
\textsc{lsc-cp-ra} is the $A=1$ case with the atom index resampled each step.

The implemented step, with drift cap $c$ set to twice the shell half-width
and integration box $\mathcal B$, is
\begin{equation}
 X_{n+1}=\operatorname{clip}_{\mathcal B}\Bigl[
 X_n+\Delta t\,\frac{b_n}{1+\Delta t\lVert b_n\rVert/c}
 +\sqrt{2\Delta t/\beta}\,\xi_n+\sum_aN_{n,a}R_{n,a}\Bigr],
 \qquad b_n=-\nabla V(X_n)+S_{\mathcal R_n,\beta}(X_n).
 \label{eq:euler-paired}
\end{equation}
Taming, clipping, quadrature, and the Euler step are outside the generator
identity; their effects are measured (certificates, refinements, and the
audit diagnostics of Appendix~\ref{app:gates}), not assumed absent.  Poisson
multiplicities are applied exactly---never truncated---and multiple events on
one atom within a step reuse its stored displacement, an $O(\Delta t^2)$
difference from full-law compound-Poisson sampling.

\section{Metric definitions}
\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{Basin occupancy, TV, and KL.}  For basin partition
$\{\Omega_k\}_{k=1}^K$ with target masses $p^\star$,
$\widehat p_k=\frac1N\sum_i\one\{X_i\in\Omega_k\}$,
$\mathrm{TV}_{\rm basin}=\frac12\sum_k|\widehat p_k-p^\star_k|$, and
\begin{equation}
 \KL(p^\star\Vert\widetilde p)
 =\sum_{k:p^\star_k>0}p^\star_k\log\frac{p^\star_k}{\widetilde p_k},
 \qquad
 \widetilde p_k=\frac{\widehat p_k+\alpha/N}{1+K\alpha/N},\quad\alpha=\tfrac12 .
\end{equation}
The target-to-empirical orientation penalizes a sampler that misses a target
basin; the Jeffreys-scale pseudocount (reported) keeps the statistic finite.

\paragraph{Free-energy-surface RMSE.}  On a fixed rectangular grid with cell
probabilities $p_i$ and volumes $|C_i|$, the reduced free energy is
$A_i=-\log(p_i/|C_i|)$.  With $\Delta_i=\widehat A_i-A^\star_i$ on the
supported cells $\mathcal I=\{i:p^\star_i\ge5/N\}$ and reference weights
$w_i=p^\star_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{RMSE}_{F/k_{\rm B}T}
 =\Bigl[\tfrac{\sum_{\mathcal I}w_i(\Delta_i-c^\star)^2}
              {\sum_{\mathcal I}w_i}\Bigr]^{1/2},
 \qquad
 c^\star=\tfrac{\sum_{\mathcal I}w_i\Delta_i}{\sum_{\mathcal I}w_i},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ free energies are compared after fitting the single additive constant
they are defined up to.  Grids use the two-dimensional active coordinates
(one-dimensional for the double well), one-half pseudocount per cell, and the
empirical mass outside the frozen grid is reported separately
(Table~\ref{tab:terminal}) rather than renormalized away.

\paragraph{IAT, ESS, MCSE, $\Rhat$.}  Per chain, the integrated
autocorrelation time uses Geyer's initial-positive/initial-monotone pairwise
sums; a constant chain is assigned $\IAT=\infty$ and zero ESS.  Independent
chains contribute $\ESS_{\rm total}=\sum_m n_m/\widehat\IAT_m$ (not
draws over mean IAT).  $\operatorname{MCSE}=s/\sqrt{\ESS}$, undefined for
zero-variance traces.  Split-$\Rhat$ is rank normalized with average ranks
for ties and Blom constant $(r-3/8)/(S+1/4)$, and the reported value is the
maximum of the bulk and folded (median-absolute-deviation) statistics;
globally constant traces give an undefined $\Rhat$.  Cost-normalized ESS
divides by wall time, gradient evaluations, potential evaluations, and
score-quadrature energy differences separately; observation functions run
outside both the timer and the evaluation ledger.

\paragraph{Passage statistics.}  Where passage times are recorded they are
right-censored at the common horizon, restricted to chains starting in the
declared home core, and summarized by the Kaplan--Meier restricted mean; the
model-dependent censored-exponential estimate, when saved, is labelled as
such.  Both are algorithmic-mixing diagnostics only.

Two domain-coverage rules were established by this campaign's failed
first attempts, and are now part of the audit design.

\emph{Rule 1: a hard-zero jump-boundary gate is unsatisfiable.}  CP-family
jumps fire independently of the state, so rare multi-jump excursions contact
\emph{any} finite integration box; measured LSC contact rates are
$10^{-4}$--$2\times10^{-3}$ per applied jump.  The gate therefore bounds
$\piB$-targeting methods at $10^{-2}$ (five to fifty times the observed
values) and \emph{records} raw \textsc{cp}, whose larger contact rate is part
of its documented stationarity defect---consistent with the refinement and
basin gates, which likewise never gate on non-targeting baselines.  Box
truncation flatters raw \textsc{cp} (Section~\ref{sec:recovery}), so
recording it is conservative for the comparison.

\emph{Rule 2: every domain-restricted diagnostic must cover the
jump-reachable set.}  The $\phi^4$ basin map was originally built on the
phase-minima square $[-2,2]^2$; a single phase-to-phase atom moves $\bar q$
by $\approx2.12$, so legitimate LSC-CP transport parks $|\bar q|$ up to
$3.3$, which the map lookup would silently clamp into boundary cells and
mislabel.  The map is now built on $[-4,4]^2$ at unchanged cell size, with
the metric bounds derived from the map object itself so the gate and the
lookup can never measure different domains.
