\section{Jump support governs which modes communicate; correctness stays automatic}
\label{sec:move3}

The triple well is the normalized Gaussian mixture with modes $(-3,0,3)$, scales $(0.5,0.75,0.5)$
and weights $(5/21,3/7,1/3)$ ($\varepsilon=0.08$), started in the left mode. It disentangles two
knobs: the stationary correction controls target fidelity, while the chosen jump \emph{support}
decides which modes exchange mass directly. The \emph{adjacent} shell connects left--middle and
middle--right; the \emph{overlong} shell connects the two outer modes directly, bypassing the
middle; a deliberately \emph{too-short} shell (scale $0.4$) does not reach a neighbor.

Table~\ref{tab:move3} shows the separation cleanly. Both corrected supports that actually reach a
neighboring mode keep the mode-TV small (adjacent $0.047$, overlong $0.015$)---correctness is
automatic for any adequate support---while the too-short corrected shell collapses to local noise
($0.375$) because no correction can create transport the jumps do not provide. The uncorrected
overlong control records the bias of omitting the correction ($0.168$ mode-TV, $0.163$ middle-mass
error), and local Langevin is fully trapped in the initial mode ($0.739$, zero all-basin coverage).
The overlong support additionally gives the fastest all-basin coverage ($0.864$ vs $0.573$),
because a single jump reaches the far mode---an illustration that support is a performance knob laid
on top of guaranteed correctness.
