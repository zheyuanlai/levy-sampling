\section{Discussion and limitations}
The method is only as useful as the jump bank supplied to it. A jump bank can increase nonlocal motion while still worsening finite-time thermodynamic estimates if the geometry, intensity, timestep, or quadrature are poorly matched. The random matched-length controls are therefore essential rather than cosmetic.

The clearest positive evidence for geometry-matched jumps comes from the trapped and high-dimensional regimes. In the four-well ablation at a barrier high enough to kinetically trap the local baselines within the compute budget, the structured minimum-to-minimum graphs recover the basin populations markedly better than the length-matched random control, and the adjacency-matched edge graph outperforms the all-pairs complete graph. In the ManyWell suite the block-flip bank, whose jump vectors match the block structure exactly, recovers per-block marginals across dimensions~8--64 where the local baselines remain entropically confined to the shallow-well basin. By contrast, on the smooth low-dimensional double-well and M\"uller--Brown landscapes the jump channel is correct but not advantageous: well-tuned local samplers (MALA, BAOAB, HMC) and parallel tempering match or exceed the jump-diffusion sampler, so these serve as validation of correctness rather than demonstrations of efficiency. This delineation---jumps help precisely when metastable transitions correspond to fixed coordinate displacements and local exchange is slow---is the main practical takeaway.

A structural limitation follows from the same principle: the fixed finite jump bank is defined by displacement vectors in the ambient coordinate space, so it is well matched to targets whose metastable transitions are fixed displacements (separable or lattice-like systems), and poorly matched to targets with continuous symmetries, where the relevant transitions are not fixed vectors. The Lennard-Jones example makes this concrete: a jump aligned to a catalogue isomer is productive only while the cluster retains that orientation, and rotational diffusion collapses its productivity to a few percent; a rotationally-augmented gated bank restores productivity but no longer fits the fixed-displacement construction and, at the temperatures where multiple isomers coexist, the cluster is not kinetically trapped, so no equilibrium efficiency gain is demonstrable. The LJ$_{38}$ experiments also clarify an important boundary of the present method. The rejection-free additive L\'evy-score correction is effective when metastable basins can be connected by symmetry-adapted jumps whose interpolation does not pass through prohibitively high-energy configurations. In rigid Lennard-Jones clusters, however, the direct Cartesian displacement between distinct packings crosses severe atom-overlap regions, causing the path-integral score correction to become exponentially small. A Metropolis-corrected, geometry-matched darting move can still solve the associated global-discovery problem by transferring configurations from the icosahedral funnel to the FCC global funnel. However, recovering the correct solid--solid equilibrium population of LJ$_{38}$ is a substantially harder free-energy problem: the icosahedral contribution is a whole-funnel entropy effect involving many low-lying minima, not a single-minimum effect. Accurate population recovery would require a full multi-center smart-darting construction with well-defined darting regions, detailed-balance-correct proposal probabilities, treatment of alignment Jacobians or volume-preserving internal coordinates, and a representative database of low-energy icosahedral minima. We therefore treat exact LJ$_{38}$ population recovery as an important direction for future work rather than a claim of the present study. The alanine-dipeptide torsion example shows the complementary positive case: on a periodic (torus) target the same construction applies verbatim through wrapped displacements and a periodic score, and geometry-matched wrapped jumps recover the conformational populations that the local baselines miss. The reported dynamics are equilibrium samplers, not physical kinetic models: nonlocal jumps deliberately alter dynamical pathways and should not be used to estimate transition rates or mechanisms without additional reweighting or kinetic modeling.
