\section{The correction is a real invariant-measure fix, not a speed trick}
\label{sec:move1}

The one-dimensional double well $V(x)=x^4/4-x^2/2$ (wells at $\pm1$, $\Delta V=1/4$,
$\varepsilon=0.125$) separates target fidelity from relaxation speed: it has an explicit Gibbs
reference, and raw CP and LSC-CP share the same symmetric shell jump law ($|r|=2$, $\lambda_0=1$)
and the same random jump times, so any difference between them is the stationary correction alone.

\paragraph{The correction removes the raw-jump bias.} Started from the left well and integrated to
$T=4.8$, LSC-CP reproduces the symmetric Gibbs target while raw CP relaxes toward a biased
stationary law and local Langevin stays trapped near its initial well (Table~\ref{tab:move1-dw}).
The correction cuts the terminal Kolmogorov (CDF-sup) error by $5.5\times$ and the density-$L^1$
error by $3.1\times$ relative to the identical uncorrected jumps.

\paragraph{The defect is a wrong invariant law, not a discretization artifact.} If the raw-CP error
were a finite-step (Euler) artifact it would vanish as $\Delta t\to0$. Starting every method
\emph{at} the Gibbs reference and measuring the residual over a fixed horizon, the raw-CP CDF-sup
error is flat at $\approx0.10$ across a factor-of-eight timestep refinement, while LSC-CP sits at
the finite-sample floor together with jump-free local Langevin (Table~\ref{tab:move1-timestep}).
The raw jumps therefore change the invariant measure; the correction restores it, and does so in
continuous time (the stationarity identity $\int A_\varepsilon\varphi\,\mathrm d\mu_\varepsilon=0$
holds for smooth compactly supported $\varphi$, verified numerically to $\sim10^{-6}$ on every jump
bank used here). This is the sense in which the method is a target-preservation construction, not a
faster integrator.
