\section{Honest scope: where the additive form breaks}
\label{sec:move5}

The additive L\'evy-score correction is target-preserving only when metastable basins are joined by
fixed coordinate displacements whose straight-line interpolation avoids prohibitively high-energy
configurations. Two Lennard-Jones cluster experiments mark the boundary.

\paragraph{Continuous symmetry (LJ$_7$, 2D).} A fixed Cartesian jump vector aligned to a catalogue
isomer is productive only while the cluster keeps that orientation. Sweeping the cluster's
orientation, the fixed jump lands at low (isomer) energy for only $2.74\%$ of angles before hitting
hard-core overlaps; a rotationally-augmented gated bank (rotated copies of the jump, gated by landing
energy) restores productivity to $100\%$. The fixed-vector construction is thus incompatible with the
cluster's continuous rotational symmetry. Moreover LJ$_7$ has no locally-trapped-yet-multi-isomer
window---at $\beta=8$ MALA already interconverts isomers $\sim30$ times per trajectory---so no method
can demonstrate a trapped-regime win there regardless of jump design. LJ$_7$ is reported as a
scope-characterizing limitation, not a positive result.

\paragraph{Steep steric barrier (LJ$_{38}$ double funnel).} The canonical metastable cluster pits
the FCC truncated-octahedron global minimum ($-173.928$, recovered exactly) against an icosahedral
funnel ($-170.99$). The straight-line displacement between the funnels crosses a $+87.7\,\varepsilon$
barrier of atomic overlaps, so at the solid--solid transition $\beta\cdot\text{barrier}\approx728$
and the rejection-free score integrand $\exp[-\beta\,\Delta V]$ is numerically zero: the additive
correction \emph{provably cannot} make such a jump target-preserving. The identical geometry-matched
displacement, applied instead as a \emph{Metropolis-corrected} endpoint dart (accept on endpoint
energy, no path integral), escapes the icosahedral funnel and finds the FCC global (FCC-funnel
fraction from the icosahedral start: local MC $0.00$ vs darting $1.00$). Geometry-matched nonlocal
moves therefore solve LJ$_{38}$ global-funnel discovery, but through the Metropolis hybrid rather
than the pure additive score; recovering the entropy-balanced funnel populations at the transition is
a whole-funnel entropy effect that needs the full smart-darting machinery (darting regions,
proposal-ratio and alignment Jacobians, a representative icosahedral-minima database), which we leave
to future work.

\paragraph{The through-line.} The method's strength and its limitation are the same principle: fixed
additive jumps are ideal when metastable transitions are fixed coordinate displacements
(separable/lattice-like systems, and CV-space torsional transitions on a torus), and inapplicable
when the relevant motion is a continuous symmetry orbit or must follow a curved, sterically blocked
path. In the latter regime the target-preservation job passes to Metropolis-corrected darting. The
practical cost throughout is that LSC-CP needs a structure-informed jump bank, unlike black-box PT or
HMC---the honesty that makes the trapped/high-dimensional wins credible.
